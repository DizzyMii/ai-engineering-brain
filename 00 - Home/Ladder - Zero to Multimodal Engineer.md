# Ladder - Zero to Multimodal Engineer

A guided walk through domain 15 (Multimodal) — with the attention and vision prerequisites pulled from domain 03 (Architectures) — for one question: *how does a model built to predict the next token learn to see an image, generate one from a sentence, and hear and produce speech?* Twenty-two steps, ordered so the story builds: the transformer and the pre-transformer vision lineage it absorbed, then how images and text get pulled into a shared embedding space, then how that space gets wired into an LLM to build a vision-language model, then how a text prompt becomes a generated image through diffusion, then the same machinery extended to audio and video, closing on a geometry quirk and an open-source war story that count as this domain's tribal knowledge. Read each note for the one thing named under it; if you can answer the self-test, you got the point and can move on. This track assumes you can already trace a token through a transformer block — [[Deep Dive - The Transformer]] in step 1 is here as review and a source of exact links, not a first introduction, so if it's genuinely new territory, budget more time on it than the pace below assumes. By the end you should be able to explain why a VLM answers from language priors instead of the pixels, why a diffusion model runs two forward passes per sampling step, and why nobody's contrastive vision-language embedding space is actually unified. For the full 25-domain map, including the rest of the Engineering Wing this track sits inside, start at [[Home]].

## Act I — The lineage (attention and the vision prehistory it absorbed)

**1. [[Deep Dive - The Transformer]]**
Extract the skeleton every later architecture in this ladder reuses without modification: a stack of pre-norm residual blocks, `x = x + Sublayer(Norm(x))`, where attention and the FFN are read/write operations on one shared residual stream rather than a sequential pipeline. Take the parameter formula $N_{params} \approx 12 \cdot n_{layers} \cdot d_{model}^2$ as your budgeting tool, and note that causal masking is what turns next-token training into one parallel forward pass instead of an RNN's token-by-token loop. Everything from here on — Vision Transformers, VLM connectors, diffusion transformers — is this exact block reused in a new context, not a new invention.
*Self-test:* Why does pre-norm keep the residual path a "clean gradient highway," and what specifically breaks at depth if you use post-norm instead?

**2. [[Concept - Attention Mechanism]]**
Extract scaled dot-product attention itself, $A = \text{softmax}(QK^T/\sqrt{d_k})$, and *why* the $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling exists — without it, unscaled dot products grow with $d_k$ and push softmax into a near-one-hot regime that starves gradient flow through everything but one position. This is the operation that, generalized to two different input streams instead of one, becomes the cross-attention that lets a diffusion U-Net read a text prompt (step 15) and lets Flamingo read an image (step 11) — same formula, different sources for Q versus K/V.
*Self-test:* If you skip the $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling entirely, what specific failure shows up in the softmax output, and why does it get worse as head dimension grows?

**3. [[Concept - Convolutional Neural Networks]]**
Extract the vision lineage ViT replaced: weight sharing (`k·k·C_in·C_out` parameters instead of `H·W·C_in·C_out`), translation equivariance, and a receptive field that grows with depth. Carry forward the non-obvious point specifically: ResNet's skip connection `y = F(x) + x` exists to solve the *degradation* problem — deeper plain networks got measurably worse at training, not just at generalizing — and that exact "make identity the cheap default" trick is the direct architectural ancestor of the transformer's residual stream from step 1, despite CNNs having spatial structure transformers don't.
*Self-test:* A 56-layer plain CNN has higher training error than a 20-layer version of the same design — why does that rule out "it's just overfitting," and what does ResNet change to fix it?

## Act II — Learning to see (vision transformers and cross-modal contrastive pretraining)

**4. [[Concept - Vision Transformers]]**
Extract patchify: a 224×224 image splits into 16×16 patches, giving $(224/16)^2=196$ tokens, each linearly projected — mechanically a strided convolution, then handed to the unmodified transformer block from step 1. Note the quadratic-cost consequence: doubling input resolution roughly quadruples patch count and multiplies attention FLOPs by roughly 16x, and that ViT's learned (not RoPE) position embeddings must be bicubically interpolated to run at an off-training resolution, a quiet source of degradation. ViT needs on the order of 100M+ training images to beat a similarly-sized ResNet, because it has none of a CNN's locality prior built in — data hunger it trades for a global receptive field from layer one.
*Self-test:* Why does doubling a ViT's input resolution cost roughly 16x more attention compute rather than 4x, and what does that force real systems to do instead of running one forward pass over a 4K image?

**5. [[Concept - Cross-Modal Representation Alignment]]**
Extract the fork this whole ladder hangs on: contrastive alignment into a shared metric space (no decoder, built for retrieval, the CLIP recipe) versus fusion into a generative LM (a decoder that can actually talk about the image, the VLM recipe) — and note that real systems compose both, with a CLIP-style tower supplying frozen features and a connector bridging into the LLM. Take the named failure mode forward: **modality collapse**, where an LLM fed unaligned vision features learns to ignore them and answer from language priors alone, detected with a counterfactual image swap.
*Self-test:* A retrieval system and a captioning system both need "cross-modal alignment" — why does only one of them need a decoder, and which paradigm does each map to?

**6. [[Concept - CLIP and Contrastive Vision-Language Training]]**
Extract the mechanism: an image encoder and text encoder map to a shared space, L2-normalized, trained on the $N \times N$ in-batch similarity matrix with symmetric InfoNCE cross-entropy — image→text and text→image averaged. Take the number that explains CLIP's whole scaling story: batch size 32,768, chosen because harder in-batch negatives directly drive representation quality, which is exactly the all-gather bottleneck SigLIP (step 7) was built to remove. Note the failure mode: CLIP behaves closer to bag-of-words-over-captions than a compositional reasoner — it struggles to tell "a red cube on a blue sphere" from "a blue cube on a red sphere" because nothing in noisy alt-text ever forced it to bind attributes to objects.
*Self-test:* CLIP reaches roughly 75% zero-shot top-1 on ImageNet with zero labeled training examples — what specific mechanism turns "classification" into "retrieval," and why does that same mechanism structurally fail at counting and spatial relations?

**7. [[Concept - SigLIP and the Sigmoid Contrastive Loss]]**
Extract the fix: replace CLIP's softmax-over-the-whole-batch loss with an independent sigmoid binary classification per (image, text) pair, removing the requirement that every negative be present for one shared normalization — SigLIP matches or beats CLIP quality at 16K batch size against CLIP's 32K, and initializes its bias term around $-10$ specifically to counter the $N$-positives-versus-$N^2{-}N$-negatives class imbalance at batch construction. Note SigLIP-So400m's practical win is resolution as much as loss efficiency — it commonly runs at 384–448px against CLIP's typical 336px, which is why it became the default 2024–25 open-VLM vision tower. The non-obvious catch: switching the loss function does not close the modality gap (step 20) — that's a property of using two independent encoders under *any* contrastive loss, not the normalization choice.
*Self-test:* SigLIP removes the global all-gather that CLIP's softmax loss requires — mechanically, why does turning the loss into a sum of independent per-pair terms make that possible?

## Act III — Vision-language models (wiring the eyes into an LLM)

**8. [[Concept - VLM Architectures]]**
Extract the three-family taxonomy every open VLM snaps into: cross-attention (Flamingo — frozen LLM, visual features as K/V), projector/prefix (LLaVA — image tokens prepended into the LLM's own token stream), and early/native fusion (Fuyu, Chameleon — one transformer trained from scratch over mixed tokens). Take the concrete cost number: one 336px CLIP ViT-L/14 image costs 576 tokens once it enters the stream in the projector family, the dominant driver of KV-cache and latency cost. The non-obvious history: the field oscillated from Flamingo's cross-attention (2022) to LLaVA's simpler projector (2023–24) and *back* to cross-attention for Llama-3.2-Vision, because once an LLM is valuable and safety-tuned, not fine-tuning it becomes a governance feature, not just a compute hack.
*Self-test:* Llama-3.2-Vision went back to Flamingo-style cross-attention after the field had settled on LLaVA-style projectors — what changed about the calculus, and what does "the LLM stays frozen" actually buy you that has nothing to do with training cost?

**9. [[Concept - Vision-Language Connectors]]**
Extract the token-count/quality tradeoff this one module controls: a linear/MLP projector keeps all 576 patch tokens (highest detail, highest cost), a Q-Former/Perceiver resampler compresses to a fixed 32–64 tokens (cheap, but an information bottleneck that costs OCR and small-object grounding), and pixel-shuffle merging (InternVL, Qwen2-VL) folds a 2×2 patch neighborhood into one token — a 4x reduction that loses less detail than a resampler at the same token count because it merges deterministically instead of learning a lossy compression. Take the two under-appreciated bugs forward: reading the ViT's *final* layer instead of the *penultimate* one loses spatial detail to CLIP's own contrastive objective, and a placeholder-token-count mismatch between the chat template and the connector's actual output silently corrupts every downstream request without ever throwing an error.
*Self-test:* "Our VLM can't read receipts" almost never gets fixed by more instruction data — which two components does the fix actually live in, and why does a fixed-size resampler make the problem structurally worse than a linear projector?

**10. [[Breakdown - LLaVA]]**
Extract the recipe that made a competent VLM a reproducible weekend project: frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 (576 tokens, penultimate layer) into a connector into Vicuna-7B/13B, trained in two stages — stage 1 aligns only the connector on 558K caption pairs with both towers frozen, stage 2 unfreezes the LLM and instruction-tunes on 158K samples. The real contribution isn't architecture, it's the data trick: text-only GPT-4, given only COCO captions and bounding boxes and never pixels, synthesized the multimodal instruction data — a teacher that never saw the modality it was teaching. Take the number that dates the recipe: SOTA on 11 of 12 benchmarks from only ~1.2M total public data points, on roughly one day of 8×A100 compute for the 7B model.
*Self-test:* GPT-4 never saw a single image while generating LLaVA's instruction-tuning data — what did it actually condition on instead, and why did that turn out to be enough?

**11. [[Breakdown - Flamingo]]**
Extract the stability trick that makes grafting new layers onto a frozen pretrained model safe: gated cross-attention layers are inserted between existing frozen Chinchilla blocks, and each gate's output is multiplied by $\tanh(\alpha)$ with $\alpha$ initialized to exactly zero — at step zero the grafted model is mathematically identical to the original frozen LM, so training starts from a known-good state instead of destructive early updates. Take the fixed-cost number: the Perceiver Resampler compresses any input — one image, five images, a video's worth of frames — to exactly 64 tokens, the opposite design point from LLaVA-NeXT's linear-in-resolution tiling. The non-obvious payoff: this same zero-init-gate move reappears later in this domain wearing different names — ControlNet's zero convolutions and DiT's adaLN-zero conditioning are the identical trick.
*Self-test:* Why does initializing Flamingo's gate parameter to exactly zero matter more than initializing it to a small random value — what specific training failure does it prevent?

**12. [[Playbook - Training a VLM from a Vision Encoder and an LLM]]**
Extract the operational sequence that turns the LLaVA recipe into a repeatable procedure: pick components and state the per-image token count *before* training starts, freeze everything but the connector for stage 1, run a wire-check that the connector's output token count exactly equals the chat template's placeholder count, then unfreeze the LLM for stage 2 instruction tuning at LR≈2e-5 with a text-only replay fraction to prevent language regression. Take the verification discipline as the real payoff: a counterfactual image-swap test and POPE-style adversarial-negative accuracy, not aggregate VQA score, are what actually confirm the model is conditioning on pixels rather than language priors.
*Self-test:* The playbook says a NaN in stage 1 "almost always" traces to one specific cause rather than the learning rate — what is it, and why would checking LR first waste your time?

## Act IV — Generating images (diffusion and latent generative models)

**13. [[Deep Dive - Diffusion Models]]**
Extract the entire training loop in one line: sample a real image, sample a timestep and noise, corrupt it to $x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon$, and regress a network to predict the $\epsilon$ that was added — $L_{simple} = \mathbb{E}[\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|^2]$, no adversarial discriminator, no mode collapse. Take the score-matching equivalence as the load-bearing fact: $\epsilon$-prediction is equivalent to estimating $\nabla_x \log p(x_t)$, which is *why* deterministic ODE samplers like DDIM are exactly correct rather than a convenient hack — they solve a different equation with identical marginals. This ladder now traces the evolution from here: DDPM to DDIM's 20–50-step deterministic sampling, to latent compression (step 14), to classifier-free guidance (step 15), each swapping one piece while keeping the forward-corrupt/reverse-generate shell.
*Self-test:* Why is a deterministic DDIM sampler not "cheating" a stochastic process into determinism — what specific mathematical fact makes it exactly equivalent rather than approximately close?

**14. [[Concept - Latent Diffusion]]**
Extract the trick that turned text-to-image from a cluster problem into a consumer-GPU problem: compress a 512×512×3 image to a 64×64×4 latent with a perceptually-trained (LPIPS plus adversarial, not pixel-MSE) autoencoder first, a ~48x reduction in elements (786,432 → 16,384), then run every step of diffusion inside that latent instead of pixel space. Take the easy-to-miss number: the latent is multiplied by a fixed scaling constant (0.18215 for SD1.x, 0.13025 for SDXL) so its variance matches what the noise schedule assumes — get the constant wrong and the model doesn't error, it just produces garbled noise. The failure mode to carry forward: the frozen VAE is a hard fidelity ceiling — no amount of denoiser improvement recovers detail the 4-channel SD1.x/SDXL autoencoder structurally can't represent, which is exactly why SD3/FLUX moved to 16 channels.
*Self-test:* A latent diffusion model produces uniformly garbled, noisy-looking output, but the training loss curve looked fine — what single silent bug should you check before assuming the sampler or the training run itself is broken?

**15. [[Concept - Classifier-Free Guidance]]**
Extract the exact mechanism behind the "CFG scale" slider: train one model to handle both conditional and unconditional generation by randomly dropping the condition to a null token about 10% of the time, then at sampling time run the model twice per step and extrapolate, $\hat\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(x_t,\varnothing) + w(\epsilon_\theta(x_t,c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t,\varnothing))$ — at $w>1$ you are deliberately sampling off the model's learned data manifold, not just "listening to the prompt more." Take the operational cost forward: CFG doubles forward passes per step, the entire reason guidance-distilled checkpoints (FLUX-schnell, SDXL-Turbo) exist. Note the portability trap: guidance scale is a per-checkpoint hyperparameter, not a universal constant — copying "CFG 7.5" from an SD1.5 workflow into SDXL or a flow-matching model routinely makes results worse, not better.
*Self-test:* Why does turning the guidance scale up past a certain point produce oversaturated, "deep-fried" images instead of just "more accurate" ones?

**16. [[Breakdown - Stable Diffusion]]**
Extract the version-over-version pattern: every SD release is the same VAE-plus-text-conditioner-plus-denoiser decomposition, with each piece independently upgraded — SD1.x/2/SDXL run a U-Net denoiser on a 4-channel latent conditioned by CLIP text embeddings via cross-attention, while SD3/FLUX swap in an MMDiT backbone, a 16-channel latent, and add T5-XXL specifically to fix legible-text rendering CLIP alone handled poorly. Take SDXL's size/crop conditioning as the reusable pattern: rather than fight the "everything looks centrally cropped" bias baked in by square-cropped training data, SDXL feeds the original size and crop offset as an explicit conditioning input, turning a data artifact into a controllable variable. Note the training-cost figure, with its evidence tier attached: SD1.4 trained for roughly 150,000 A100-hours (~$600K, Mostaque's own unaudited claim) on a LAION-2B aesthetic subset — the number that made "download and fine-tune a real text-to-image model on one GPU" possible for the first time.
*Self-test:* SDXL conditions the model explicitly on crop offset and original image size — what specific training-data bias is that fixing, and why is "condition on the bias" a better fix than "remove the bias from the data"?

## Act V — Audio and video (the same machinery, two more modalities)

**17. [[Concept - Audio Spectrograms and Mel Features]]**
Extract the four-step pipeline that turns a waveform into something a transformer can eat: frame into 25ms windows with a 10ms hop (fixing 100 frames/second), FFT each frame, warp the linear-Hz magnitude spectrum through a mel filterbank matmul ($m = 2595\log_{10}(1+f/700)$) down to 80–128 bins, then log-compress. Take the number that dates the design: it's a fixed, non-learned, decades-old feature extractor that Whisper (step 18), released in 2022, still chose over a learned front end, because the mel warp already encodes a perceptual prior a learned frontend would otherwise have to rediscover from data. Note the failure mode that costs the most debugging time: phase is discarded at the FFT step, so reconstruction is inherently lossy, and a silent sample-rate mismatch — feeding 44.1kHz audio to a 16kHz-trained model — doesn't error, it just makes every mel bin map to the wrong physical frequency.
*Self-test:* Why can't you losslessly reconstruct audio from a log-mel spectrogram no matter how good your model is, and what does a production TTS pipeline do to work around that?

**18. [[Breakdown - Whisper]]**
Extract the actual contribution, since the architecture is a stock encoder-decoder transformer: 680,000 hours of noisy, weakly-labeled web audio across 96 languages, filtered by heuristics rather than hand-annotated, on the bet that scale-plus-noise beats small clean data for robustness. Take the multitask mechanism forward: special prefix tokens (`<|transcribe|>`, `<|translate|>`, a language-ID token) act as a task selector for one shared decoder — functionally a chat template for audio — unifying transcription, translation, and language ID into one set of weights. Take the failure mode by name: silence or non-speech input gives the encoder a weak conditioning signal, and the decoder — underneath, a real language model — falls back on its LM prior and hallucinates fluent, entirely fabricated text rather than outputting nothing, which is why production systems bolt on voice-activity detection before the model ever sees the audio.
*Self-test:* Whisper hallucinating fluent text on silent audio isn't really a bug in the traditional sense — what is the decoder actually doing when it does that, and what's the standard production fix?

**19. [[Concept - Video Generation]]**
Extract why naive frame-by-frame diffusion fails — flicker, morphing, lost identity — and the fix: compress video with a causal 3D VAE (~8x8 spatial, ~4x temporal, "causal" meaning it only looks backward so a clip can be extended without re-encoding everything already generated), patchify the spacetime latent, and use *factorized* spatial-plus-temporal attention instead of full 3D attention. Take the concrete FLOPs number: at $t{=}32, h{=}w{=}32$ full attention costs $N^2 \approx 1.07 \times 10^9$ pairwise interactions per layer, factorized attention cuts that to roughly $3.5\times10^7$ — about 30x cheaper, which is why every production video model interleaves spatial and temporal attention blocks instead of doing joint 3D attention. Note the debugging discipline worth stealing: isolate VAE reconstruction error from diffusion sampling error first — if a held-out clip flickers after just an encode-decode roundtrip with no diffusion involved, no amount of retraining the diffusion transformer will fix it.
*Self-test:* A generated video clip's colors and identity drift the longer it runs — name two structurally different causes this could be, and which one you'd rule out first by testing the VAE alone.

## The unicorn tier — where multimodal still breaks

**20. [[Concept - The Modality Gap in Contrastive Models]]**
Extract the fact that should recalibrate every intuition from Act II: CLIP's image and text embeddings do not merge into one space — they live in two separate cones with a persistent offset, and matched-pair cosine similarity is typically only ~0.2–0.3, far below the ~0.5–0.9 you see within a single modality. Take the mechanism forward: a randomly initialized network maps *any* input into a narrow cone (the "cone effect"), image and text towers land in *different* cones before a single gradient step, and the InfoNCE loss only enforces *relative* ordering within a batch — a rigid translation of the entire text cone changes nothing about which pair ranks highest, so there is zero gradient pressure to close the gap. Note the practical sting: this is why a VLM connector has to learn a genuine cross-cone bridge rather than a resize, and why switching CLIP for SigLIP (step 7) doesn't fix it — the gap is a property of two independent encoders under any contrastive loss, not a normalization artifact.
*Self-test:* CLIP can hit state-of-the-art zero-shot retrieval accuracy while its image and text embeddings never spatially overlap — how is that possible, and what does it reveal about what the contrastive loss actually optimizes?

**21. [[Gotchas - Vision-Language Models]]**
Extract the ranked failure catalog an engineer actually ships against: the blind VLM (answers don't change when you swap the image), object hallucination (measured by POPE's random/popular/adversarial split — a big adversarial-versus-random gap is the fingerprint of prior-driven confabulation), image-token budget blowup (4 tiles plus a thumbnail at 576 tokens/tile is ~2,880 tokens for one image), and the placeholder/chat-template mismatch that desyncs positions with no crash and no error, just silent quality collapse. Take the frozen-encoder ceiling as the deepest one: if the vision encoder never unfreezes, its features — including the high-norm outlier tokens from register-token artifacts — are the perceptual ceiling for the entire system, and no amount of LLM-side SFT moves fine-grained perception past that ceiling.
*Self-test:* A team spends months tuning SFT data to fix a VLM's OCR problem with no improvement — per this catalog, which two components should they have checked first, and why would more instruction data never have fixed it?

**22. [[Lore - The Stable Diffusion Release and Its Aftermath]]**
Extract the flywheel mechanics: open weights (August 2022, ~150,000 A100-hours) plus a cheap composable customization method (DreamBooth, then LoRA at a few megabytes per style) plus a distribution hub (Civitai) built an ecosystem no single company could steer — and once Stability had handed out the weights, it had no leverage to re-close the loop when SD3's 2024 restrictive license triggered backlash, since the community and the original CompVis researchers simply regrouped as Black Forest Labs and shipped FLUX.1 instead. Take the second lesson as the one that outlives the drama: your training data's provenance is a permanent inherited liability, not a footnote — LAION-5B made Stable Diffusion possible and then, when Stanford researchers found CSAM in it in December 2023, made every model trained on it a legal exposure, alongside the still-unresolved Getty and Andersen copyright suits.
*Self-test:* Stability released SD3 under a more restrictive license in 2024 and the ecosystem didn't shrink to comply — it relocated to FLUX.1 instead. What does that reveal about where the actual value sat: the model weights, or something else?
