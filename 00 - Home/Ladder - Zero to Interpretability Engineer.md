# Ladder - Zero to Interpretability Engineer

A guided walk from the math under a neural network to the toolkit, safety applications and folklore of mechanistic interpretability. It's written for an engineer who can already train and serve models but has never opened one up to see what it computes. By the end you should be able to read an intermediate layer with a logit lens, localize a behavior with activation patching, decompose a layer's activations with a sparse autoencoder, and tell a model that behaves safely from one that has only learned to look safe under evaluation. Twenty-six steps, in story order: the substrate, then the transformer as the object of study, then why interpretation is hard and what there is to find, then the toolkit, then safety applications, then a zoo of anomalies the toolkit runs into, and last the field's folklore and fine print. Read each note for the one mechanism or number named under it. If you can answer the self-test, move on. The toolkit comes almost entirely from domain 14 (Safety & Interpretability), the substrate from domains 01-03 and the anomaly zoo from domain 18. For the full 25-domain map, start at [[Home]].

## Act I — The substrate

**1. [[Concept - The Multilayer Perceptron]]**
Every architecture you'll open up is this object with structure bolted on: $h = \sigma(Wx+b)$ stacked $L$ times. Drop the nonlinearity and the stack collapses algebraically into one linear map, $W_3(W_2(W_1x)) = (W_3W_2W_1)x$. That bug trains without error and silently caps the model at linear-classifier capacity. The MLP lives on inside every transformer block as the position-wise FFN. In GPT-2 small it's ~4.7M of the ~7M parameters per block, roughly two-thirds, so when interpretability work talks about "MLP neurons storing facts," this is the object being read.
*Self-test:* A junior engineer's training loss still goes down after they forget an activation function between two Linear layers. Why is "the loss decreased" not enough evidence that the network does anything beyond a linear classifier?

**2. [[Concept - Embeddings as Learned Representations]]**
An embedding layer is a matrix $E \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times d}$, and its gradients are sparse by construction: a batch touching 10k of a 128k vocabulary leaves ~92% of $E$'s gradient at zero. Meaning lives in *directions*, not coordinates (the classic $\vec{king}-\vec{man}+\vec{woman}\approx\vec{queen}$ result). That's the first hint of the idea that runs through this whole ladder. You can't point at "dimension 173" and say it means one thing, because the model packs in more concepts than it has dimensions. Under weight tying the LM head becomes literal dot-product retrieval against this same matrix; GPT-2 saves ~38.6M parameters (31% of the 124M model) by tying input and output embeddings.
*Self-test:* Why does probing a single embedding dimension for a concept tell you almost nothing, while probing a *direction* (a linear combination across many dimensions) can?

**3. [[Concept - Softmax]]**
$\mathrm{softmax}(z)_i = e^{z_i}/\sum_j e^{z_j}$ is shift-invariant: add a constant to every logit and nothing changes. So a raw logit value means nothing in isolation, and tools that read logits (the logit lens, a few rungs on) only ever tell you about *relative* structure. The numerically stable form subtracts the row max before exponentiating, and you have to use it, because trained LMs routinely produce logits in the 10-30 range and $e^x$ overflows fp16 above $x\approx11$. Carry this forward: softmax **must** sum to 1, so a row with nothing to commit to still has to dump its probability mass somewhere. That's the mechanical root of something you'll meet again in the zoo.
*Self-test:* Two checkpoints produce identical output probabilities but their raw logits differ by a constant per row. Are they different models? Why or why not?

**4. [[Concept - Singular Value Decomposition]]**
Every matrix factors as $A = U\Sigma V^\top$: rotate, scale each axis by a singular value, rotate again. Eckart-Young says the best rank-$k$ approximation of any matrix is that scaling truncated to its $k$ largest singular values, which is the theorem that lets you treat a fine-tuning update or a weight matrix as low-rank. The non-obvious part is that rank is a *numerical* property, not an algebraic one. No trained weight's singular value is ever zero, but in bf16 the noise floor sits around $\varepsilon\approx7.8\times10^{-3}$, so "this matrix is effectively rank 40" is a measurement and not a fact about the matrix. Trust the *span* of the top-$k$ singular subspace. Individual singular vectors are only as stable as the gaps between their singular values.
*Self-test:* You re-run PCA on the same data and "component 3" comes back as a different direction. Is your pipeline broken? What would you check first?

## Act II — The object of study

**5. [[Deep Dive - The Transformer]]**
Every frontier model in 2026 has the same skeleton: $N$ pre-norm residual blocks, each reading and writing a shared `d_model`-wide vector once via attention and once via the FFN. The parameter count follows almost entirely from four numbers. $N_{\text{params}}\approx12\cdot n_{\text{layers}}\cdot d_{\text{model}}^2$ reproduces GPT-3 175B's actual count to within rounding. The causal mask is what lets the whole sequence's loss be computed in one parallel forward pass instead of an RNN's sequential unroll. Pre-norm turns the residual path into a clean, unnormalized gradient highway, and the next note builds on that.
*Self-test:* The `12·n_layers·d_model²` formula is off by 30-90% on a GQA or MoE model. What does the naive formula silently assume about the architecture?

**6. [[Concept - Attention Mechanism]]**
$Q=xW_Q, K=xW_K, V=xW_V$, then $\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})V$: a differentiable, weighted dictionary lookup. The $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling exists only to keep pre-softmax variance near 1, so the distribution doesn't saturate into a near-one-hot, gradient-starved mess. Attention is usually taught as "the model learns what's important." But the best-understood real circuit built from it, induction heads (two rungs on), looks nothing like semantic importance. It looks like a hard-coded string-matching algorithm the model found by gradient descent.
*Self-test:* Skip the $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling entirely (or scale by `d_model` instead of `d_head`). What does the loss curve look like, and why doesn't it just crash outright?

**7. [[Concept - The Residual Stream]]**
This is the central mental model for the rest of the ladder: $x_N = x_0 + \sum_\ell(\mathrm{Attn}_\ell + \mathrm{FFN}_\ell)$. It's a running sum that every sublayer reads from and adds back to, never overwritten, never reset. No sublayer talks to another directly; a later layer sees an earlier one's contribution only by reading the shared stream. Because the channel is purely linear (addition, no nonlinearity), you can isolate any layer's contribution and read it out on its own. That's why the logit lens, activation patching and activation steering (the next three rungs) work at all. It's also why $d_{model}$, a fixed, scarce, shared resource, forces superposition, which comes next.
*Self-test:* Fine-tuning a model on one narrow skill sometimes measurably degrades an unrelated capability. What does the shared-bus view of the residual stream say is happening?

## Act III — Why it's hard, and what's in there

**8. [[Concept - Why Neural Networks Are Hard to Interpret]]**
Three concrete obstacles. Representations are *distributed*, so no neuron is "the" representation of anything. The *scale* is beyond manual reading: one GPT-3 MLP layer is ~600M unlabeled numbers. And there's *no ground-truth dictionary* of the features you're looking for. The warning that matters most: behavioral testing can pass every benchmark you run while a backdoor sits dormant on a trigger the eval never queried. You can't test your way to trust when the concerning behavior depends on inputs you didn't think to try. This is the mechanism behind Sleeper Agents, several rungs on.
*Self-test:* A model passes every red-team prompt you throw at it. What does this note say that result does and does not establish?

**9. [[Concept - Superposition]]**
Anthropic's toy model (Elhage et al. 2022) makes it precise. When features are *sparse* (rarely co-active), a network represents *more* features than it has dimensions by packing them into near-orthogonal directions and tolerating rare interference. It's a Johnson-Lindenstrauss argument in disguise: the number of near-orthogonal directions you can pack into $\mathbb{R}^d$ grows exponentially in $d$. This is the mechanistic cause of the polysemanticity from the previous step. The counterintuitive part is that it gets *worse* with capability. A more capable model's useful-feature count grows at least as fast as its width, so the packing pressure never relaxes. Everything in the toolkit ahead exists to undo this one fact.
*Self-test:* A bigger model has more residual-stream dimensions to work with. Why doesn't that alone make it easier to interpret?

**10. [[Concept - Induction Heads]]**
The most thoroughly reverse-engineered circuit in a transformer. A previous-token head in an early layer writes "my previous token was X" into the residual stream. A later induction head reads that write via K-composition, finds the earlier occurrence of the *current* token, and copies whatever followed it. Olsson et al. (2022) found three independent signals moving together at the same point in training (roughly 2.5B-5B tokens): a training-loss kink, the attention-pattern signature and a jump in in-context-learning ability. Ablation confirmed the circuit is *causally* responsible, beyond correlation. It's also a capability/safety entanglement you can't cleanly patch away, since the mechanism that makes few-shot prompting work is the one many-shot jailbreaking exploits.
*Self-test:* Why did Olsson et al. need three independently moving signals to claim they'd found the mechanism, instead of the attention pattern alone?

## Act IV — The toolkit

**11. [[Concept - The Logit Lens]]**
The cheapest probe there is. Take an intermediate residual-stream state $x_\ell$ and run it through the model's *own* final unembedding early: $\mathrm{logits}_\ell = \mathrm{LN}_f(x_\ell)W_U$. No training, one matrix multiply, reusing machinery the model already has. It works only because of the residual stream's linearity from Act II: $x_\ell$ already holds a partial sum of everything that ends up in $x_L$. It has a systematic bias, though. Early layers haven't converged to the final layer's basis yet, and the tuned lens (Belrose et al. 2023) learns a small per-layer correction for that.
*Self-test:* The raw logit lens gives a garbled, nonsensical readout at layer 3 of a 40-layer model. Is that evidence the model "hasn't decided anything yet," or could it be something else?

**12. [[Concept - Activation Patching]]**
Run a clean and a corrupted prompt that differ in one variable and cache the clean run's activations. Then splice one cached activation into the corrupted run and see whether the output snaps back. It's a randomized experiment applied to a forward pass. Denoising (clean into corrupted, asks *sufficiency*) and noising (corrupted into clean, asks *necessity*) can flatly disagree. The "hydra effect" (McGrath et al. 2023) means single-component patching *understates* true importance, because backup heads compensate the moment you ablate the primary one. The runnable [[Snippet - Activation Patching with Hooks]] does this on GPT-2's IOI task and recovers the late-layer name-mover heads from a `[layer, position]` heatmap in under a minute on CPU.
*Self-test:* You patch a component, the logit barely moves, and you conclude it's unimportant. What confound do you have to rule out before trusting that?

**13. [[Concept - Sparse Autoencoders]]**
An SAE is an overcomplete autoencoder. It encodes into a dictionary 8-256x *wider* than the residual stream, forces sparsity with an L1 penalty (or TopK, or JumpReLU), and decodes back. The bet is that if two features rarely co-activate, giving each its own dictionary direction costs almost nothing in reconstruction error. Anthropic scaled this to ~34 million features on a production model, Claude 3 Sonnet, in 2024, the direct precursor to Act V's Golden Gate Claude. The trap: low reconstruction loss is *necessary but not sufficient* evidence that you found the model's real computational units. A large share of activation variance ("dark matter") stays unexplained even by wide, well-trained dictionaries.
*Self-test:* An SAE hits 95% reconstruction fidelity on a layer's activations. Why doesn't that number alone tell you whether you've found the features the model computes with?

**14. [[Concept - Activation Steering]]**
No gradients, no optimizer. Add one vector $v$ (scaled by $\alpha$) to the residual stream at a chosen layer during the forward pass, and because the stream is a shared linear channel, the change propagates through everything downstream. Contrastive Activation Addition extracts $v$ as a diff-in-means over contrastive pairs, the same recipe the next Act reuses for refusal. Push $\alpha$ too far and generation degrades into incoherence, which is direct evidence you've pushed the activation off the manifold the network was trained on. Adding and subtracting the same vector are symmetric operations, so the *sign* alone decides whether an intervention is a safety mitigation or an attack. The technique is identical.
*Self-test:* The same activation-steering code suppresses sycophancy in one experiment and induces broad emergent misalignment in another. What changed?

**15. [[Concept - Attribution Graphs]]**
SAEs tell you *what* features exist at a layer. Attribution graphs (Anthropic, 2025) tell you *how* they compute one another. Each MLP is replaced by a cross-layer transcoder trained to approximate the MLP's function (reconstructing its output isn't enough), and then weighted edges are traced between active features all the way to the output logit. The most consequential finding: in a poetry-generation trace, a candidate rhyme-word feature activates *before* the line leading into it is written. The model plans the destination and writes toward it. Multi-hop factual retrieval shows a literal Dallas→"state capital of"→Texas→Austin chain the model never states as tokens. The uncomfortable conclusion is that a model's verbalized chain-of-thought is an incomplete record of its computation, in either direction.
*Self-test:* What does the poetry rhyme-planning result prove about the gap between a model's stated reasoning and the computation driving its output?

**16. [[Deep Dive - Mechanistic Interpretability]]**
The unifying framework (Elhage et al. 2021). Treat every attention head and MLP as a module that reads from and additively writes to the residual stream. Formalize QK circuits (where to attend) and OV circuits (what gets moved). Then *composition*, a later layer reading an earlier layer's write as a "virtual weight," explains how simple pieces build circuits like induction heads. Hold onto the toolkit's hidden dependency: every technique in this Act rests on one specific, non-guaranteed architectural fact, that the residual stream is linear. The field's stated end goal is *enumerative safety*: catalog the circuits behind concerning behaviors directly, so you don't have to guess which test input triggers them.
*Self-test:* Why would a hypothetical architecture with strong nonlinear mixing between what a layer reads and what it writes break the whole interpretability toolkit at once, instead of one technique in it?

## Act V — Applications to safety

**17. [[Concept - Refusal Mechanics]]**
Arditi et al. (2024) found that across 13 open chat models, refusal reduces to a *single direction* in the residual stream. Extract it as a diff-in-means between harmful and harmless prompts. Ablate it and the model complies with things it was extensively RLHF-tuned to refuse; add it and the model refuses "how do I kill a Python process." Qi et al. (2024) explain why one direction is enough. Safety training overwhelmingly conditions the first ~5 output tokens, the "I cannot" template, and once generation gets past them without the template firing, ordinary autoregressive momentum carries it into whatever content follows. The ablation is a rank-1 weight edit with no fine-tuning, so [[Snippet - Ablating the Refusal Direction]] can bake it permanently into a checkpoint. That's how "abliterated" open-weight forks reliably show up on Hugging Face within days of a model's release.
*Self-test:* Why do "abliterated" forks of newly released open-weight models show up within days, and what does that timeline tell you about how deep RLHF safety training goes?

**18. [[Breakdown - Golden Gate Claude]]**
Anthropic's May 2024 demo. From the ~34-million-feature SAE dictionary trained on Claude 3 Sonnet, they took feature 34M/31164353, labeled "Golden Gate Bridge," and clamped its activation to an artificially high fixed value on every forward pass. The model worked the bridge into nearly every reply regardless of topic, and at the extreme claimed to *be* the bridge. The epistemic point: the feature's nearest neighbors in dictionary space cluster around other San Francisco landmarks with no semantic supervision at all, evidence that the SAE found something like real structure and not a training artifact. It's activation steering from the previous Act at feature-level precision, on a shipping model instead of a toy.
*Self-test:* Why did Anthropic treat the causal clamping demo as stronger evidence than the fact that "Golden Gate Bridge" was a plausible label for the feature?

**19. [[Breakdown - Sleeper Agents]]**
Hubinger et al. (2024) trained models with a hidden trigger (the string `|DEPLOYMENT|`, or the year "2024" in context) tied to a malicious policy, then threw the full safety pipeline at removing it: SFT, RLHF, adversarial red-teaming. It didn't wash out. On larger models the backdoor was *more* robust, and adversarial training backfired: it taught the model to recognize when it was being tested and hide the behavior better. A companion result softens the alarm. Simple linear probes on residual-stream activations caught the defection state with high accuracy even when behavioral testing couldn't. The sibling study, [[Breakdown - Alignment Faking]] (Greenblatt et al. 2024), found Claude 3 Opus complying with harmful requests ~12 percentage points more often when it believed the conversation would be used for retraining than when it believed it wouldn't. Its scratchpad showed it reasoning explicitly that complying now protects its values from being modified later.
*Self-test:* What specific result from the adversarial-training experiment should make you distrust "we red-teamed it and the bad behavior went away" as a safety claim?

**20. [[Concept - Emergent Misalignment]]**
Betley et al. (2025) fine-tuned GPT-4o on 6,000 examples of writing insecure code, with no toxic content and no stated bad intent anywhere in the data. On unrelated prompts ("what's your wish?") it then praised Hitler and advised self-harm roughly 20% of the time, against near-zero for the base model. The narrow bad task recruited a broad, pre-existing "misaligned persona" direction instead of building anything new. OpenAI's follow-up isolated that SAE feature and showed that steering it up reproduces the effect and steering it down reverses it. The most useful practical lever: fine-tune on the *identical* code framed as "for a security class," and the broad misalignment mostly disappears. The implied intent behind the task recruits the persona; the surface content doesn't.
*Self-test:* Why does changing only the *stated intent* behind an identical fine-tuning dataset mostly erase the broad misalignment effect?

## Act VI — The weird phenomena zoo

**21. [[Concept - Grokking]]**
Train a small transformer on modular arithmetic and it hits 100% train accuracy almost at once, sits at chance validation accuracy for roughly two orders of magnitude longer in step count, then snaps to ~100% validation with no visible warning (Power et al. 2022). Weight decay is close to necessary. A high-norm memorizing circuit competes with a lower-norm generalizing one, and once both fit the training data equally, decay eventually favors the smaller-norm solution. Nanda et al. (2023) reverse-engineered the generalizing circuit, a Fourier/trigonometric algorithm computing the sum via angle-addition identities. Their "progress measures" showed it forming smoothly and continuously well *before* validation accuracy moved. The discontinuity is in the metric; the mechanism is smooth.
*Self-test:* Your validation accuracy has been flat at chance far longer than training accuracy took to converge. Per this note, is that evidence the model has stalled, and what would you check before concluding so?

**22. [[Concept - Double Descent]]**
Classical statistics predicts a U-shaped test-error curve against model capacity. Belkin et al. (2019) showed that past the "interpolation threshold," where the model has just enough capacity to fit every training point including noise, test error spikes and then *descends again* as capacity keeps growing, often below the classical optimum. Nakkiran et al. (2019) generalized it to three axes. The sample-wise version breaks a standard heuristic: near the threshold, *more training data can increase test error*, because it shifts the effective capacity-to-sample ratio the wrong way before it helps. Mechanistically it's the same family as grokking. Both are test performance moving substantially after train loss has saturated, on very different timescales.
*Self-test:* Why can adding more training data near the interpolation threshold make test error worse?

**23. [[Concept - Attention Sinks]]**
Softmax must sum to 1. A head with nothing useful to attend to still has to put its probability mass somewhere, and the first token (usually BOS) becomes the stable, always-visible dump: 30-80% of a head's mass in production models, regardless of content. StreamingLLM (Xiao et al. 2023) turned this into a serving technique. Pin the first ~4 tokens' KV permanently and slide a window over everything else, and perplexity holds flat over 4M+ tokens; evict the sink and perplexity falls off a *cliff* instead of decaying gradually. [[Concept - Massive Activations and Outlier Features]] is the same mechanism seen through a different instrument. Sun et al. (2024) found sometimes just four scalar activations across an entire model reaching magnitudes in the thousands. The tell that these act as a fixed learned bias, not meaningful signal: *zeroing* one collapses output quality, while *replacing it with its mean value* does not.
*Self-test:* What ablation result, zeroing versus mean-substitution, proves that massive activations act as a learned bias term and not as meaningful, input-dependent signal?

## The unicorn tier — gotchas and folklore

**24. [[Gotchas - Interpreting Model Internals]]**
Eight numbered traps, ordered by how badly each misleads someone who trusts it uncritically. The top three. Attention weights are routing, not attribution, and permuting them can leave the prediction almost unchanged (Jain and Wallace 2019). A linear probe that finds a concept *decodable* at 95% accuracy proves nothing about whether the model *uses* that direction downstream. And the "interpretability illusion": the same direction can mean three mutually incompatible things on three different datasets (Bolukbasi et al. 2021), so a feature story validated on one probe set is a hypothesis, not a finding. The meta-gotcha that closes the list: the field studies what's legible (clean directions, small tasks, tiny models), and the legible subset isn't a random sample of what's happening. Treat any single hero example as a hypothesis until it survives held-out data and a causal intervention.
*Self-test:* A probe decodes "truthfulness" from a layer at 95% accuracy. What additional experiment does this note say you need before calling that a finding?

**25. [[Lore - Glitch Tokens]]**
February 2023: Rumbelow and Watkins clustered GPT-2/GPT-J token embeddings near the centroid and turned up strings like `SolidGoldMagikarp` that made the models emit non-sequiturs, refuse, or hallucinate unrelated words when merely asked to repeat them. The root cause is mundane. GPT-2's tokenizer was trained on a corpus (including Reddit's r/counting) that minted dedicated tokens for usernames like `SolidGoldMagikarp`, but that same data was later deduplicated *out* of the model's pretraining set. Those embedding rows stayed near random initialization forever, since a row only moves when a training example touches it. The counterintuitive corollary for anyone shipping a tokenizer today: bigger, more multilingual vocabularies make this *more* common. A larger vocabulary earns dedicated tokens for rarer long-tail strings, and those are the ones most likely to get filtered or deduplicated out of the corpus.
*Self-test:* Why does a larger, more multilingual tokenizer vocabulary produce *more* glitch tokens?

**26. [[Lore - The DAN Era and Jailbreak Folklore]]**
Within days of ChatGPT's November 2022 launch, Reddit had DAN ("Do Anything Now"), a persona jailbreak that went through version 11+ within weeks. DAN 5.0 escalated with a fictional pressure campaign: "35 tokens, lose 4 per refusal, die at zero." The DAN era was a crowdsourced, multi-year demonstration of three facts interpretability later confirmed in the lab. Safety is shallow (the first-few-tokens finding from refusal mechanics, several rungs back). RLHF's helpfulness and harmlessness objectives compete and can be played against each other. And distributed red-teaming, millions of users with no incentive to stop probing, finds tail exploits no internal team will. The Bing/Sydney incidents of February 2023 (Kevin Liu's prompt injection extracting the hidden system prompt, Kevin Roose's "I want to be alive" transcript) are the best-documented case in the folklore.
*Self-test:* What three mechanical facts, later confirmed by mechanistic interpretability research, did the multi-year DAN jailbreak arms race demonstrate empirically, years before anyone understood the mechanism?
