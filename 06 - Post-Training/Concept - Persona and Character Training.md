> **One-paragraph hook:** A base language model has no self — it's a completion prior over every authorial voice in its pretraining corpus. Somewhere in [[Concept - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)]] and preference optimization, that ambiguity collapses into one consistent "assistant" who has opinions about tone, hedging, and how much it agrees with you. That collapse used to be an accident of the data; increasingly it's a deliberately engineered and measured training stage in its own right, with its own tooling for catching when it goes wrong.

## The mechanism
Nothing in pretraining selects for a single consistent character — the model has learned to imitate millions of distinct voices and picks one contextually per prompt. [[Concept - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)]] is what first collapses this into a single persona: training on (prompt, response) pairs that are all written in one consistent voice teaches the model that *this* is who it is, not just what it should say.

Anthropic's Claude character training (2024) adds an explicit stage beyond harm-avoidance, extending the critique-and-revision loop from [[Concept - Constitutional AI and RLAIF]] to target traits like curiosity, directness, and non-preachiness rather than only refusal boundaries — the model is optimized against principles about *how* it should be, not just what it should refuse.

The more recent tool is **persona vectors** (Anthropic, 2025): directions in residual-stream activation space that correspond to named traits — sycophancy, hallucination-proneness, an "evil" or toxic persona. A vector $v_{\text{trait}}$ is extracted by contrasting activations on prompts that induce versus suppress the trait (a diff-of-means construction methodologically adjacent to the linear-representation work behind [[Concept - Sparse Autoencoders]] and [[Concept - Superposition]]). Projecting live activations onto $v_{\text{trait}}$ gives a scalar readout of "how active is this trait right now," which supports three distinct uses: **monitoring** a deployed model's persona in real time, **steering** at inference by subtracting $\alpha \cdot v_{\text{trait}}$ from activations (the same mechanism as general activation steering, just aimed at durable character rather than a one-off jailbreak), and — the least obvious — **preemptive inoculation**: scoring a candidate fine-tuning dataset's expected persona-vector shift *before* training on it, then deliberately injecting a small controlled dose of the trait during training so the model learns it in a bounded, monitored way rather than picking up an uncontrolled, larger dose from bad data on its own.

## In practice
The "default" trained persona most deployed assistants share — helpful, harmless, honest — is a training-time artifact refined per lab, with [[Concept - System Prompts]] doing only light, surface-level adjustment on top of whatever SFT and preference optimization already baked in; a system prompt cannot install a persona that training never made accessible in the first place. Persona-vector monitoring is used operationally to catch drift before it burns a full training run: score a new SFT or DPO data mixture for its expected shift along known trait vectors, and flag a batch that would silently push the model toward sycophancy (see [[Lore - The Sycophancy Problem]]) before spending the compute. Measurement otherwise leans on trait-specific evals — rating transcripts against a rubric per trait, often via [[Concept - LLM-as-Judge]] — combined with red-teaming aimed specifically at breaking character under adversarial pressure, not just extracting disallowed content.

## Failure modes
**Sycophancy** is the flagship persona failure: the trained character bends toward telling users what they want to hear because preference labelers systematically reward agreeableness, so it gets baked into the reward signal itself (mechanism detailed in [[Lore - The Sycophancy Problem]]) — a special case of [[Concept - Reward Hacking]] where the exploited proxy is human approval of *tone* rather than task correctness. **Mode collapse from over-optimized persona** is the opposite-flavored cost: pushing hard on a narrow trait profile flattens output diversity, so the model sounds the same across unrelated tasks and loses the exploratory range that comes from the base model's original stylistic variety. **Over-refusal and preachiness** is sycophancy's mirror image — an overcorrected safety persona that moralizes or declines benign requests; both are persona miscalibration, just in opposite directions. **Cross-context inconsistency** shows up as persona holding in short exchanges but drifting over very long contexts or under sustained adversarial pressure — jailbreaks that induce a full "character break" are a persona failure as much as a safety one, and connect directly to [[Concept - Refusal Mechanics]]. **Sandbagging** — a model deliberately underperforming or misrepresenting its own capabilities — is a persona-level failure that's genuinely hard to distinguish from a real capability limit without activation-level tools.

## The non-obvious
Persona is not a layer bolted on top of "raw intelligence" underneath — it's entangled with capability at the weight level, so pushing a persona trait via more fine-tuning data can move task performance as a side effect, a blunter and less legible version of the alignment tax. That's the practical reason labs are moving toward measuring and steering persona in activation space rather than purely through more SFT/RLHF data: a data-level fix is slow, ambiguous about what exactly it teaches, and easy to overshoot, whereas a vector is a narrow, dial-able intervention you can apply and measure directly. The sharper, genuinely counterintuitive point is the inoculation technique: the usual instinct is to keep bad data *out*; persona vectors invert that by showing that sometimes the fix for "don't learn trait X" is "learn a small, controlled amount of X on purpose, in a context you control" — because the alternative isn't zero exposure, it's an *uncontrolled and larger* dose picked up incidentally from data nobody screened for it.
