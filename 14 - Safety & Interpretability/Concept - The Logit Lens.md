> **One-paragraph hook:** A transformer only officially "answers" once, at the final layer, but the [[Deep Dive - Mechanistic Interpretability|residual stream]] it builds that answer from is available at every layer along the way. The logit lens is the cheapest possible interpretability probe: take the intermediate residual stream at layer $\ell$, run it through the exact same final normalization and unembedding the model would use if that were the last layer, and read off a provisional next-token distribution. No training, no intervention, one matrix multiply against machinery the model already has — and it turns out that provisional distribution is often a legible, steadily sharpening guess, giving you a free window into *when* in the forward pass an answer actually forms.

## The mechanism

The technique, due to nostalgebraist (2020, LessWrong), is literally the model's own last step applied early. If the model normally computes final logits as $\text{logits} = \text{LN}_f(x_L)\,W_U$ from the final residual stream $x_L$ and unembedding matrix $W_U$, the logit lens computes:

$$\text{logits}_\ell = \text{LN}_f(x_\ell)\,W_U, \qquad \ell < L$$

— substituting an earlier layer's residual stream $x_\ell$ into the same final [[Concept - RMSNorm and LayerNorm|LayerNorm]] and [[Concept - Softmax|softmax]] pipeline the model uses at layer $L$. This only produces something meaningful because of the additive, linear structure of the residual stream (see [[Deep Dive - Mechanistic Interpretability]]): $x_\ell$ already contains a partial sum of everything that will eventually make up $x_L$, so projecting it early through the model's own final readout gives a legitimate, if incomplete, version of the same computation rather than nonsense from an arbitrary intermediate tensor.

What it reveals, run layer by layer across a prompt, is that prediction is not a single event at the last layer but an iterative refinement across depth: early layers' logit-lens readout is often dominated by detokenization noise — resolving surface-level artifacts like [[Concept - Embeddings as Learned Representations|subword/BPE]] boundary ambiguity rather than saying anything about the eventual answer; middle layers start promoting semantically related candidates without committing; and late layers sharply promote the actual output token, often several layers before the literal final layer. Locating the specific layer at which the correct answer first becomes the top logit-lens candidate is now a standard diagnostic for "where in the network does this fact live" — complementary to, and much cheaper than, running full [[Concept - Activation Patching|activation patching]] to establish the same thing causally.

The raw logit lens has a systematic bias, though: $W_U$ and $\text{LN}_f$ were only ever trained to work well on $x_L$, so applying them to $x_\ell$ implicitly assumes intermediate layers already live in the same basis as the final layer, which is not exactly true. The **tuned lens** (Belrose et al. 2023) fixes this by learning a small per-layer affine probe — a translator $x_\ell \mapsto A_\ell x_\ell + b_\ell$ — trained (cheaply, by distillation against the model's actual final-layer output) to correct each layer's specific bias before applying the shared unembedding. The tuned lens is consistently more faithful than the raw lens, especially in early layers where the basis-mismatch problem is worst, at the cost of a small amount of per-model training instead of zero.

## In practice

Beyond pure diagnosis, the layer-wise readout has direct engineering uses. It guides **early-exit** decoding schemes and some [[Concept - Speculative Decoding|speculative-decoding]] draft strategies, where the question "has the answer already stabilized by layer $\ell$" determines whether computation can stop early for that token without changing the sampled output. It also underlies **DoLa** decoding (Chuang et al. 2023), which contrasts the logit-lens-style distribution from an early/premature layer against the final-layer distribution and amplifies the difference — the intuition being that factual knowledge tends to sharpen in later layers, so the contrast suppresses low-confidence, hallucination-prone tokens that an early layer would have favored. Because it costs one matrix multiply against activations you'd typically cache anyway, the logit lens is usually the *first* thing a practitioner runs before reaching for anything from the [[Concept - Sampling and Decoding Parameters|decoding]] or [[Deep Dive - Mechanistic Interpretability|circuits]] toolkits — cheap enough to run on every layer of every prompt as a matter of habit, unlike patching or SAE feature extraction, which require deliberate setup.

## Failure modes

- **Over-reading garbled early-layer output.** In the first several layers, the readout is frequently a jumble of unrelated or oddly specific subword fragments, because early residual-stream states genuinely have not yet been refined into anything resembling a final-layer-basis representation. Detection: treat early-layer logit-lens output as informative about *whether* the model has settled on an answer yet (it usually hasn't) rather than *what* it's thinking — don't narrate a story from noise.
- **Basis mismatch in architectures with strong per-layer rescaling.** Models with aggressive RMSNorm scaling or certain rotary-embedding interactions can maintain intermediate representations that are rotated or rescaled away from the final unembedding basis more than the raw lens assumes, producing systematically misleading (not just noisy) readouts rather than merely uninformative ones. Detection: compare raw-lens output against the tuned lens on the same model — a large, consistent gap between the two indicates the raw lens is actively misleading here, not just weak.
- **Treating the projected distribution as the model's literal belief.** The logit lens is a linear projection of an intermediate state, not a claim that the model "is thinking token X" at layer $\ell$ in any deeper sense — the same caution that applies to reading attention weights as explanations applies here: a legible number is not automatically a faithful one. Detection: corroborate a logit-lens finding with a causal check ([[Concept - Activation Patching|patching]]) before treating "layer $\ell$ is where fact X lives" as established rather than suggestive.

## The non-obvious

The logit lens works at all only because the residual stream and the unembedding matrix share a basis that the model is implicitly incentivized to maintain across layers — a byproduct of the additive residual structure, not a guaranteed property of any possible architecture. That means the technique's reliability is itself evidence about the model, not just a neutral measurement tool: a model whose raw-lens readout is unusually garbled at a layer where the tuned lens is clean is telling you something about how that layer's representation is rotated relative to the output basis, which is precisely the gap the tuned lens was built to quantify and correct. Taken further, nothing architecturally prevents a model from routing some computation *off* the shared basis entirely — hiding it from a lens that only ever looks through the front door of the final unembedding — which is part of why the field treats the logit lens as a fast first look, never the final word, and reaches for [[Gotchas - Interpreting Model Internals|causal, structural methods]] whenever the stakes justify the extra cost.
