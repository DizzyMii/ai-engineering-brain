> **One-paragraph hook:** The textbook picture of a loss landscape is a scatter of isolated valleys, one per local minimum found by a different training run — but empirically, distinct SGD solutions are connected by simple low-loss paths, and flatter solutions generalize better than sharp ones. This geometry is not a curiosity: it is the reason weight averaging, Model Soups, and merging fine-tunes with SLERP or TIES actually work instead of producing garbage, and it's why sharpness-aware training and lottery-ticket rewinding behave the way they do.

## The mechanism
Loss-landscape geometry is the connective tissue behind several of this domain's strangest results — [[Concept - Double Descent]]'s non-monotone test-error curve and [[Concept - Grokking]]'s delayed generalization are both, at bottom, statements about which basin gradient descent lands in and how that basin's shape changes with capacity or training time. Garipov et al. 2018 ("Loss Surfaces, Mode Connectivity, and Fast Ensembling") showed that two independently-trained SGD solutions, naively thought to sit in separate basins separated by a high-loss barrier, are in fact connected by simple curves — Bezier curves or piecewise-linear paths — of near-constant *low* loss. The straight line between them is high-loss, but a slightly bent path isn't. This rewrites the "many isolated minima" intuition: the landscape is far more connected than a straight-line loss barrier would suggest.

**Linear mode connectivity (LMC)** (Frankle et al. 2020) sharpens this: two networks are linearly mode connected if the *straight-line* interpolation between their weights (not just some clever curve) stays low-loss. Two networks trained from the *same* initialization with different data order or seed become LMC only after a short early "burn-in" period — the network has to become stable to SGD noise first. This is the same stability point that makes [[Concept - The Lottery Ticket Hypothesis]]'s late-rewinding trick work: rewinding a lottery ticket mask to init fails on harder tasks, but rewinding to an early step (0.1–7% of training), right around where LMC kicks in, succeeds.

**Permutation symmetry and Git Re-Basin** (Ainsworth et al. 2022) extends this to networks trained from *different* initializations, which normally show a high loss barrier between them — because hidden units in a layer can be permuted without changing the function the network computes, two networks solving the same problem can have their features arranged in unrelated orders. Ainsworth et al. show that if you first permute one network's hidden units to align with the other's, the interpolation barrier between the aligned networks largely vanishes: "LMC modulo permutation." The suggestion is striking — there may be essentially one big loss basin once you quotient out the relabeling symmetry, not a landscape of many small ones.

**Flatness and generalization** is the complementary axis. Hochreiter & Schmidhuber (1997) argued on minimum-description-length grounds that flat minima — regions where loss stays low under weight perturbation — generalize better than sharp ones, because a flat solution needs fewer bits to specify. Keskar et al. (2016) gave the empirical hook everyone cites: large-batch settings in the [[Concept - The Training Loop]] tend to converge to sharper minima that generalize worse than the flatter minima small-batch SGD finds, tying a training hyperparameter directly to landscape geometry — the same optimizer-generalization tension documented as folklore in [[Lore - The Adam vs SGD Generalization Wars]].

**[[Concept - Sharpness-Aware Minimization]]** (Foret et al. 2020) operationalizes the flatness argument directly: instead of minimizing $L(w)$, minimize the worst-case loss in an $\epsilon$-neighborhood,
$$\min_w \max_{\|\epsilon\| \le \rho} L(w + \epsilon)$$
approximated with one ascent step (find the worst-case perturbation) followed by a descent step at that perturbed point. This roughly doubles per-step cost but reliably improves generalization; variants (ASAM, GSAM) adapt the neighborhood shape and matter especially for ViT training.

## In practice
This geometry is the load-bearing justification for a cluster of production techniques: weight averaging along a single training trajectory (SWA, EMA checkpoints) works because nearby-in-time checkpoints are trivially mode-connected. Model Soups (Wortsman et al. 2022) average multiple independently fine-tuned checkpoints of the *same* pretrained model and get free accuracy gains over any single member — this only works because fine-tunes of a shared base checkpoint stay in the same basin. Merging techniques that combine differently fine-tuned models (SLERP, TIES, DARE) live one step further out on this same continuum, and their success or failure is a direct empirical readout of how mode-connected the source checkpoints actually are. Where checkpoints aren't mode-connected at all — different architectures, or bases too dissimilar to interpolate — [[Concept - Knowledge Distillation]] is the fallback route to a single compact model, at the cost of a full training run instead of a free average.

## Failure modes
Sharpness is not an intrinsic property of a minimum — it's **reparameterization-dependent**: Dinh et al. (2017) showed you can rescale a network's weights (using ReLU's positive homogeneity, for instance) so a flat minimum looks arbitrarily sharp in the rescaled coordinates, without changing the function computed at all. Any claim that "flatness predicts generalization" needs a scale-invariant notion of flatness (e.g., relative to weight norm) or it's measuring an artifact of parameterization, not geometry. The permutation-alignment story is also uneven across architectures: it is cleanest for MLPs and CNNs, where "permute the hidden units" is a well-posed matching problem; it gets murkier for attention layers, where permutation symmetry interacts with head structure and positional information in ways that are less cleanly solved.

## The non-obvious
The lottery ticket rewind, model merging, and grokking's delayed generalization all sit on the *same* landscape geometry, and seeing that unifies three results that look unrelated on the surface. [[Concept - Grokking]]'s late generalization is, in the Omnigrok framing, a story about the optimizer eventually finding its way from a memorizing region of weight-norm space into a lower-norm, flatter, generalizing region — the same weight-decay-driven pressure toward flat, low-norm solutions that [[Concept - Adam and AdamW]]'s decoupled weight decay applies during ordinary training. Once you frame grokking, lottery tickets, and merging as three different observations of "how connected and how flat is the basin a given training run lands in," the otherwise-surprising fact that Model Soups just *works* stops being surprising. Why overparameterized nets generalize at all remains genuinely unsolved — flat-minima and mode-connectivity arguments are leading partial answers, not a proof, and the question is catalogued as open in [[Reference - Open Problems in LLM Engineering]].
