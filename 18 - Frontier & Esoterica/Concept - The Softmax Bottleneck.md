> **One-paragraph hook:** Every autoregressive language model computes its next-token distribution the same way — take the final hidden state, dot it against an embedding matrix, softmax the result — and that single architectural choice imposes a hard mathematical ceiling on which conditional distributions the model can ever express, no matter how well it's trained or how much data it sees. This is the softmax bottleneck: a rank limitation baked into the output layer's linear algebra, not a training failure, and it's the reason some tokens are structurally un-generatable at greedy decode regardless of model quality.

## The mechanism
Yang et al. 2018, "Breaking the Softmax Bottleneck: A High-Rank RNN Language Model," made the argument precise. A language model's pre-[[Concept - Softmax]] logits are computed as $L = H W^\top$, where $H \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$ stacks the $n$ distinct context hidden states the model can produce and $W \in \mathbb{R}^{|V| \times d}$ is the output embedding matrix over a vocabulary of size $|V|$. The matrix of true log-probabilities the model needs to represent — one row per context, one column per vocabulary token — is a function of $L$, and because $L$ is the product of two matrices sharing an inner dimension $d$, it has $\mathrm{rank}(L) \le d$.

The catch: natural-language next-token log-probability matrices are empirically **high-rank**. Different contexts genuinely require qualitatively different-shaped distributions over the vocabulary — not just different peaks, but distributions that can't be produced as a low-dimensional linear combination of a shared basis. When $d \ll |V|$ (a hidden dimension of a few thousand against a vocabulary of tens of thousands, itself shaped by [[Concept - Byte-Pair Encoding]]'s choice of vocabulary size), there provably exist context-conditional distributions the model **cannot represent for any setting of its weights**. This isn't underfitting — more data or more training steps cannot fix it, because the ceiling is a property of the matrix factorization's rank, not of how well the factors are chosen.

The paper's proposed fix is **Mixture of Softmaxes (MoS)**: instead of one softmax over one context vector, compute $K$ separate context vectors from the hidden state, run a softmax over each, and mix:

$$P(y \mid c) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \pi_k(c) \cdot \mathrm{softmax}(h_k(c)^\top w_y)$$

where $\pi_k(c)$ are context-dependent mixing weights. A single softmax is fundamentally rank-$d$-limited, but a **log of a mixture** of softmaxes is not similarly rank-bounded — mixing in probability space (not logit space) is exactly what escapes the linear-algebra constraint. MoS measurably improves perplexity on PTB and WikiText benchmarks, at a real cost: computing $K$ softmaxes per prediction step is roughly $K$ times the output-layer compute of a single softmax, which is why it hasn't seen wide adoption in frontier-scale production serving where output-layer compute is already a meaningful fraction of decode-time cost.

A sharper and more practically relevant corollary comes from Demeter et al. 2020, "Stolen Probability: A Structural Weakness of Neural Language Models." Because logits are dot products between a hidden state and a *fixed* embedding vector per token, any token whose output embedding sits strictly inside the convex hull of the other tokens' embeddings can **never be the single highest-scoring logit for any hidden state** — its neighbors always "steal" its probability first, geometrically. Some tokens are structurally un-generatable at greedy decode, an artifact of the embedding geometry entirely independent of training quality. This directly shapes how [[Concept - Sampling and Decoding Parameters]] and greedy/beam decoding behave in practice, and it's a standing structural contributor to the broader pattern of degenerate outputs studied under [[Concept - Neural Text Degeneration and Repetition Loops]].

## In practice
Modern frontier LLMs mostly *escape* the bottleneck's worst practical bite simply by making $d$ large: hidden dimensions of 4096–16384 are large relative to the effective rank most natural-language distributions actually need, so the ceiling is rarely the binding constraint. Untied, large output heads — a separate, independently-learned unembedding matrix rather than one shared with the input [[Concept - Embeddings as Learned Representations]] — also help by giving the model more room to place token vectors in useful configurations rather than compromising with a matrix that also has to serve as the input embedding. The bottleneck resurfaces where it's easy to forget it exists: small models (edge/mobile-scale LLMs with $d$ in the low hundreds), aggressive weight tying between the input and output embeddings (common exactly where you're trying to save parameters, which is precisely where $d$ is already tightest), and any deliberately low-rank output projection introduced to save memory or compute at the output layer.

## Failure modes
- **Pathological over-smoothing in small or heavily tied-weight models.** Symptom: the model struggles to sharply separate near-synonyms or closely related next-token candidates — probability mass smears across a cluster of plausible tokens instead of committing. Cause: with $d$ small relative to $|V|$, the achievable logit matrix is too low-rank to express a sharply peaked distribution for every context that needs one. Detection: probe minimal-pair contexts (near-identical prompts that should produce very different sharp distributions) and check whether the model's top-token probabilities are suspiciously flat across them.
- **Greedy decode silently skipping structurally disadvantaged tokens.** Symptom: certain vocabulary tokens almost never appear as the argmax output even in contexts where they're clearly the best answer. Cause: the stolen-probability geometry places their embedding inside the convex hull of competitors. Fix: this is not fixable by more training on the same architecture — sampling-based decoding, larger untied output heads, or embedding-geometry changes are the available levers.
- **Interaction with final-layer normalization and temperature.** The expressible sharpness of the output distribution interacts with logit temperature and the final [[Concept - Softmax]] call; naively tuning temperature to "fix" a flat-looking distribution can mask a genuine rank-limitation rather than resolve it.

## The non-obvious
The practically important takeaway isn't "add Mixture of Softmaxes to your architecture" — MoS's compute cost means almost nobody ships it at frontier scale, and large hidden dimensions already make the rank ceiling a non-issue for most contexts in practice. The takeaway that survives at any scale is the stolen-probability corollary: because output logits are dot products against fixed embedding vectors, there is a **permanent, training-independent geometric property** of which tokens can win at greedy decode, unrelated to how good the model's understanding of the context is. This is a standing argument for treating "the model assigned token X near-zero probability" with more suspicion than usual when X's embedding might simply be structurally disadvantaged — a case where the fix lives in decoding strategy or embedding-space design, not in more training.
